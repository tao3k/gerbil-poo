#+TITLE: Selective generalized component construction

* Native composition

This advances the generalized-prototype future work. Computation expressions
(~dependency-analysis.ss~) infer dependencies from child expressions, including
presence observations and both conditional branches. Declaration sources
(~generalized-graph.ss~) support define/remove/set-base/support and reject cycles
or unsupported reads before construction. Builder and materialized caches
(~construction-cache.ss~) retain successes, source failures and real absences.

~StagedPublication.methods.construction<-cache~ specializes the existing model's
~.construct / .refresh~ hooks. The Boolean cyclic solver remains its default;
general component graphs require a valid DAG schedule. Cooking, typed generics,
negative watches, contracts and joint approval reuse one shared finish boundary.

* Reuse and invalidation

Reuse requires exact program/base identity and clean transitive dependencies.
Equal outputs or version labels cannot authorize reuse. Changed support/order is
conservative. A dependency-ordered pass materializes each shared node once; clean
slots never invoke a builder or resolver. Source capture uses direct lookup tables
for active keys while preserving latent code/base for later support edits.

An identical captured source retains its lookup and schedule in constant source-
level work (one private cache record and shared traces). Changed sources still
capture and scan supported keys. ~reschedule~ reuses checked order/reads when
support and every complete footprint agree; changed edges fall back to full
cycle/unsupported validation. Value/program invalidation is independent: a new
method body still rebuilds its cone even when its reads are unchanged. Private
transient tables reuse the standard hash library. Values, source objects and
callbacks must remain stable and pure. Open POO metadata and arbitrary extension
callbacks do not carry dependent kernel proofs. Extensions must declare their
reads; actual undeclared provider accesses are refused.

Accepted releases refresh generics from closed per-service inputs. Equal inputs
under the identical model retain exact warm certificates; changed inputs rebuild
only that generic, including registration and completed-candidate negative checks.
ContractFamily also projects descriptor inputs and retains wrappers with the same
executor, while revalidating current service support.
Calls retain the exact component cache and recheck the actual service-cache
successor. A refused release retains the identical previous version, including
its component values and warm service caches. External effects are outside this
value rollback contract.

* Shared settlement example

~examples/construction-workflow.ss~ reuses ConstructionWorkflow's fifteen keys:
input feeds freight/cover/audit, tax/risk merge at settlement, invoice/audit merge
at release, and the absence of blocked feeds total. It composes the existing C4
ServiceBundle and its quote/reserve/cancel metadata and negative-watch rules.

- Input 30: 15 evaluated, total 32.
- Invoice adjustment: 3 evaluated / 12 reused, total 41. Reservation, cancellation
  and guarded Bank quotation all capture the revised component values.
- Shared input 60 after that adjustment: 11 evaluated / 4 reused, total 67.
- Discount presence change invalidates risk and all dependent joins.
- Policy-only repricing: 0 evaluated / 15 reused, even with a builder refusing all
  nodes. The family and contracts still rebuild and undergo joint approval.
- Cycles, builder refusal, blocked capability, forbidden dispatch and late joint
  refusal cannot publish a mixed version.

The component arithmetic aligns with the Lean settlement example. Existing
Scheme C4 quote policies are reused; full dependent-type or quote-policy parity
is not claimed.

* Evidence and output

Tests exercise refusing builders, shared-node call counts, independent numerical
expectations, cold/refresh comparisons, failed/absent reuse, latent source support,
new same-result programs, cycles, undeclared reads and cross-service rollback.
Lean adds ~Partial.step_reused_builder_independent~ and
~Scheduled.invalidates_reader~ and checks shared-root revision under a builder
rejecting every node outside its eleven-node cone.

~just benchmark-metadata~ measures 75 phases / 600 observations. Component phases
include source capture, scheduling and cache preparation; release phases include
fresh services/contracts and admission. Existing 90-second compile, 20-second
measurement and 1-GiB heap limits remain. Allocation is unavailable across GC or
negative counter deltas; signed diagnostics are retained.

Complete raw results stay under ignored ~.build/metadata/~, with concise source-
bound repository summaries. See [[file:performance/results.org][benchmark output policy]] for native result storage and reproduction. No new versioned JSON
or Lean checkout dependency is introduced.

* Stable and dynamic joint approval

The native model's ~.readmit~ method defaults to full ~admit~. ServiceBundle
specializes it to ~cache-admit~: every new publication runs all three business
probes, release/ceiling checks and the budget; each call checks the actual
successor budget. Methods, contracts, cooked view and policy remain immutable
through dispatch-cache insertion. Stable probes therefore retain their meaning.
Custom overrides must justify every omitted check; an arbitrary cache-dependent
or effectful predicate must continue to use full admission.

Differential controls run identical cold/hit/cross-service calls under full and
factored approval, comparing results, refusals and all cache sizes. A second
service exceeding the joint budget still refuses without leaking tentative state.
Schedule controls cover equal footprints with new bodies, changed edges, cycles
and unsupported reads. Lean's ~Scheduled.rebase~, ~Cache.retain~, family definition
and observation preservation, and ~Snapshot.invokeRetaining~ state the respective
proof obligations. They do not constitute compiler/runtime refinement.

Native same-process controls include full/retained scheduling, recaptured/same-
source zero-builder preparation and full/factored cold/warm joint admission.
Release measurements include construction, generic rebuilding, contract binding
and full fresh approval. These finite observations do not prove universal speed.

* Selective service-family revision

~GenericFamily.Projection~ captures a finite immutable input value per generic;
~expand~ constructs its closed policy/view environment. Native
~methods.refresh<-projection~ derives build/prepare/refresh using those inputs
and the existing seed/method/watch callbacks. Source/model identity is mandatory.
A changed source reconstructs everything even when input values compare equal.
The private family carries warm certificates and source contexts; only invoke
can create a warm successor. Context/prototype values and callbacks remain pure
and stable. Context equality is an optimization premise, not a version stamp.

The staged model now calls family prepare/refresh in its existing atomic chain.
Unchanged entries share their original cache; the service table is copied only
on the first changed entry. New generic registration/negative refusal or late
joint refusal returns the identical old version. Cold construction uses the same
projected environments. Default models conservatively reconstruct all services.

ServiceBundle projects quote capacity/price/extension flags, reserve
capacity/release/shift/signature, and cancel invoice/shift/negative flags. A price
revision rebuilds quote while retaining exact warm reserve/cancel objects. An
invoice revision rebuilds cancel only. A no-op revision retains all three. Joint
cache approval counts retained warm caches; tightening a limit can refuse even
when no generic was rebuilt. Negative assumptions are checked on every changed
generic. Changing the approval program falls back to full approval.

Stable quote/cancel business conclusions can transfer only from the identical
admission source, identical actual service and identical contract descriptor
(absence included), with matching expected view inputs. Current ceiling/release
and exact successor cache budget are always checked. Reservation contracts/wrappers now retain through ContractFamily when their
source/inputs and executor agree; exact service/contract identity permits retaining
the reservation business conclusion as well. Arbitrary custom admission
or contract changes cannot inherit this shortcut.

Runtime controls compare projected and direct constructors over 36 view settings
and six actual calls each, preserve sibling cache identity, and test changed code,
new budgets, changed admission, negative conflicts and atomic refusal. Native
matched release controls compare selective versus forced cold preparation for
no-op, price-only and invoice-only revisions. Complete 400-row data remains in
ignored build output. Lean has Source.asModel/projection, Certified.transport,
Partial.refresh_retained, and stageRefresh/publishRefresh with refusal identity.
This is executable alignment with explicit premises, not compiler refinement.

* Contract-family preparation

~contract-family.ss~ supplies native ~Source.~ with keys/project/construct and
derived ~.prepare / .refresh~. Only projected inputs reach construction. Source
identity changes force reconstruction; equal names/results cannot authorize reuse.
Every requested key is checked against current service support before projection
or construction; duplicate names and invalid descriptors refuse preparation.

Private contract and invoker tables share unchanged entries, with independent
copy-on-write on first descriptor/binding change. An executor change reuses the
descriptor but binds a new wrapper; key removal rebuilds finite support and cannot
leave stale invokers. Wrappers receive the current publication snapshot as an
argument and resolve services there, including freshly replaced generics. Custom
legacy contract callbacks fall back to ordinary preparation rather than being
ignored by the projection mixin.

ServiceBundle projects the reservation contract on capacity. Price/invoice-only
revisions retain both descriptor and wrapper. Existing joint admission transfers
the reserve observation only from the same admitted source and same actual
service/contract identities with unchanged expected capacity. New ceilings,
release conditions and the actual cache budget are still checked. Normal calls
always enforce input/output/relational predicates; retained wrappers never bypass
checks. Contract removal, changed constructor, missing service and late refusal
retain the previous version on failure. Source exceptions remain distinct from
business error values through the existing metadata wrapper.

Native tests count actual construction/wrapping/probe calls, exercise input and
logic refusal, replacement services through retained wrappers, independent
executor rebinding, contract add/remove, duplicate/missing keys and legacy
contract overrides. Lean ContractFamily certifies origins/bindings and exact
zero-build/zero-bind retained traces, current-state invocation, missing-service
refusal and atomic joint refusal; its reserve/cancel example changes invoice
while retaining reserve. Matched native phases isolate cold/retained preparation,
changed inputs, rebind-only work, and complete no-op/price releases with cold
versus retained contracts. These are finite observations, not verified Scheme
allocation/compiler/runtime claims. Complete data/binaries remain ignored outputs.

Contract release pairs use 128 operations on both sides after an initial 8-op
natural-GC price regression. The concise performance summary retains that
negative observation; initial data/log/source and final measurements stay ignored.
The follow-up changed batch size only, preserving production source and all
compile/time/heap bounds. It does not establish a universal performance guarantee.

* Shared heterogeneous contract contexts

~ContractFamily.methods.inputs<-shared~ derives ~.inputs~ from ~shared-input~,
~prepare-shared~ and ~project-shared~. Only the closed immutable shared context
reaches preparation. A private source-bound batch retains the prepared lookup
when that complete context is equal; changed contexts prepare once for the whole
family. Independent ~Source.~ implementations keep ordinary per-key projections.
The ~family-prepared?~ trace reports actual shared preparation, not skipped calls
for sources that do not implement the shared protocol. An empty/default source
still has the original semantics. Current support and duplicates are checked for
all keys before any shared preparation, descriptor construction or wrapper binding.

~examples/shared-contracts.ss~ composes this mixin with existing reserve/cancel
constructors, typed generic projections and staged publication. Price revisions
retain both contracts and wrappers and skip both stable business probes. Capacity
and invoice changes jointly rebuild affected descriptors; invoice-only changes
retain reserve. Missing service, changed source, shared preparation failure and
joint admission refusal are checked by native tests. Old snapshots and warmed
certificates remain intact after refusal. Ordinary invocation always checks metadata;
dynamic cache/ceiling/release approval is not transferred by the shared context.

Lean ~ContractFamily.SharedProjection~ uses the corresponding structure for closed input,
preparation and projection. ~refresh_project / cached_source_project~ establish
fresh-input equivalence for every heterogeneous key, ~refresh_retained~ proves
zero prepare calls on unchanged input, ~refresh_cost~ bounds calls by one, and
~retain_origin~ preserves contract origin when only sibling inputs change.
These laws reuse existing binding/publication laws and assume immutable pure
sources. They do not verify Scheme allocation or runtime execution.

The native matrix now has 56 phases / 448 rows. Shared/independent input and
complete price/joint publication pairs use 128 operations equally on both sides,
including preparation, construction, binding, admission and actual checked calls.
The small two-contract joint changed-input case regressed (348.684 versus 338.934
microseconds under natural GC); this negative result remains in the concise
summary. An initial new benchmark oracle incorrectly omitted quote from the
capacity dirty set and refused the run; only that expectation was corrected.
No incomplete/oracle-failed run is admitted as a performance result.

* Costed selection and invariant contract prototypes

The isolated pre-change study measured descriptor construction at about 95 us per
reserve/cancel pair, wrapper creation at 0.16 us, generic refresh at 114 us and
joint approval at 14 us (natural GC). These are separate batch medians, not additive
parts of one timed request. Shared/independent joint publication differed only
about 0.3 percent in this sample; the prior 2.9 percent difference remains recorded.

Reservation and cancellation now derive from private native Contract prototypes
using ~.cc~ and a fresh ~meaning~ closure. Invariant input/output Type predicates and
Formula nodes are reused; changed capacity/invoice never retains old interpretation.
Ordinary metadata checks and negative dispatch watches still run. Tests check shared
predicate/formula identities, distinct descriptor identities, changed interpretations,
invalid arguments, source replacement and dynamic warm-cache refusal.

~ContractFamily.PreparationCosts.~ mirrors Lean ~PreparationCosts~. Native prices
~prepare-cost / shared-cost / direct-cost / selection-cost~ are nonnegative integers.
~choose-preparation~ selects direct only if its modeled saving covers selection;
ties use shared. ~methods.inputs<-selection~ derives the existing closed-input
protocol, and ~family-preparation~ records direct/shared/retained/independent.
The existing shared protocol remains available. Prices affect performance choice,
not support checks or admission. The direct callback must equal shared projection
for all closed inputs; Lean ~direct_source_eq~ establishes that equality for the
model, and the native heterogeneous controls check the corresponding functions.

The selected example uses the two-field context directly instead of allocating
an index. Its ~workflow-model~ composes the existing 15-node settlement DAG,
cooked interface, typed generic projections, two contracts and joint admission.
An invoice dependency revision evaluates 3 nodes and retains 12, then rebuilds
both affected contracts. A price-only follow-up retains both bound wrappers and
warm reservation; a zero cache budget still refuses that actual successor.

Lean ~Contract.withMeaning~ preserves input/output/rule, and both example
specializations equal the original contracts for every parameter.
~PreparationCosts.direct_covered / publication_direct_bound~ include selection
alongside unchanged construction/binding/generic/probe counts. The fallback bound
charges selection overhead explicitly. ~template_amortized~ includes once-only
validation and needs an explicit break-even premise; it does not declare cold
process startup free or prove native compiler/runtime performance.

The final native matrix has 75 phases / 600 rows. It adds stage isolation,
selector/direct/shared controls, original unfactored constructors and a full
settlement-DAG matched pair. All new pairs use 128 operations on both sides,
four rotating rounds and both GC regimes under the existing 90s compile/20s
measurement/1-GiB bounds. Initial profiling and earlier regressions stay ignored
and are described in the concise summary. No raw results or archives are committed.

Final matched controls show invariant specialization reducing descriptor construction
from 105.441 to 17.223 us (natural), and from 95.922 to 19.402 us (collected).
Complete binding isolation, including current-state adapters, costs 0.395/0.402 us;
joint admission costs 13.754/15.926 us. These standalone phases are not additive.
The shared settlement pair improves 380.461 -> 290.941 us (natural) and
358.344 -> 272.215 us (collected), with identical construction/admission work.

The dynamic selector is experimental, not the default source strategy. Its final
natural complete-joint median is 255.750 vs independent 222.422 us (~15 percent
regression); collected is 249.598 vs 248.648 us (~0.4 percent regression).
Per-round selected/independent GC counts are 1/0, 21/0, 31/31 and 24/32; the raw
CPU median includes those different GC histories. This is not evidence that
selector arithmetic causes the full gap, nor evidence that direct is uniformly
faster. The initial 584-row optimized study and all negative controls remain
ignored and described in the summary. The robust gain is invariant descriptor
specialization; actual runtime strategy still needs matched workload measurements.

Lean now makes this restriction explicit with ~withCollections~ and
~publication_collection_bound~: collection-count budgets accompany the core-cost
bound. ~collection_budget_bound~ allows an excess only if core savings cover its
priced charge. An executable counterexample shows that one extra collection can
reverse a primitive saving. Native GC observations cannot be dropped when using
the model to recommend a strategy. These are conditional cost laws, not a collector
or compiler verification, and no Lean results are embedded into this package.

* Prepared selectors and fresh-process pairing

~PreparationPlan.~ corresponds to Lean ~PreparationCosts.Plan~. It captures a
choice for canonical immutable ~keys~, returns that result for matching size,
and invokes the original selector for different arity. ~methods.inputs<-plan~
composes it with the existing closed shared-input protocol. Dynamic selection
captures the immutable choose function with ~std/func compose1~ (fixed arity);
planned selection uses its bound function directly, avoiding repeated generic
lookup. The example exports ~planned-source / planned-model / planned-workflow-model~
as optional compositions, not a universal performance default.

Support and duplicates are checked before cold planning is forced. Changing
source/plan invalidates source-bound descriptors normally. Tests count real
selector executions, handle removal/reintroduction, verify current interpretation
and exercise the 15-node invoice/release/total revision, subsequent pricing,
wrapper/warm-cache retention and actual budget refusal. Logical approvals and
negative checks keep their original provenance requirements.

Lean ~Plan.select_eq / hit / miss~ preserve every choice and count actual
selection paths. ~PlanCosts.total_amortized~ charges setup, the first selector,
support guards and fallback calls. A one-off plan may cost more. The collector
budget is still an extra premise, and no native heap or compiler verification
is asserted. ~paired_delta~ decomposes a single observed pair; independent
medians do not have that algebraic identity.

~just benchmark-metadata-paired~ uses four scenarios (selector, full contract
family, atomic joint revision, shared settlement workflow) and adjacent controls:
independent vs uncaptured, uncaptured vs captured dynamic, captured vs planned.
Each side starts a new native process with one-character strategy codes, loads
all four controls in the same order, runs four fixed warmup passes for every
control, and measures only one side. The caller balances side order ABBA over
four rounds and rotates pair positions. Both natural and collected controls keep
all samples, GC CPU/counts, prefix counters and signed allocation diagnostics.

This is 192 fresh child processes, each <=20s / 1 GiB; compilation <=90s and the
aggregate driver <=90s. These are separate scope bounds, not a widened 20s
single-child limit. Prefix GC differences are reported, not silently filtered;
matching counts alone do not establish heap identity. CPU-minus-GC includes
construction, dispatch and admission too. Report paired deltas before taking
medians; never subtract arbitrary phase medians to estimate selector cost.
Source/binary changes, missing pairs, wrong side/order, incorrect allocation
admission, incomplete output and nonfinite metrics fail admission. Failed runs
stay incomplete and ignored. No raw matrix, archive or versioned JSON is stored.
